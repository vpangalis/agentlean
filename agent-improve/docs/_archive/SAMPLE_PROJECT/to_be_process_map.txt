TO-BE PROCESS MAP — Supplier invoice receipt to posting
Case IMPR-2026-0E5, Reduce invoice handling errors
Status: DESIGN. Not yet implemented. Describes the intended future state.

SCOPE
In:  Supplier invoices received through email PDF, supplier portal, and EDI,
     EU entity, from receipt through to posting.
Out: Purchase-order creation, supplier master data, disputes already in the
     escalation queue.

STEP 1 — INTAKE
Supplier submits an invoice. Every channel is subject to the same mandatory
check at the point of entry: the invoice must carry a valid purchase-order
reference before it is accepted into the AP queue. An invoice without one is
returned to the supplier at intake with the reason stated, rather than being
accepted and corrected later by AP.
Today this check exists only downstream, and only on the portal and EDI paths.

STEP 2 — CHANNEL MIGRATION
Tail suppliers submitting by email PDF are onboarded onto the supplier portal,
highest volume first. The email PDF path remains open but is the exception
route, not the default. Target: the majority of invoice volume arrives through
portal or EDI.

STEP 3 — VALIDATION
Automated validation on receipt: PO reference present and open, line values
within tolerance against the PO, tax code resolvable from the supplier record,
and duplicate detection against invoices already in the queue. Anything failing
validation is held in an exceptions queue and never enters the manual stream.

STEP 4 — MATCHING AND CODING
Clean invoices match and code without manual intervention. AP staff work the
exceptions queue only.

STEP 5 — APPROVAL ROUTING
Routed by value and cost centre against a maintained approver matrix.
Escalation on age, so nothing sits unactioned.

STEP 6 — POSTING
Posted and released to the Treasury payment run.

CAPACITY AT MONTH-END
Month-end volume peaks are levelled rather than absorbed. Invoice submission
cut-offs are communicated to suppliers, and approval capacity is planned
against the expected peak rather than the average week.

WHAT GOOD LOOKS LIKE
- Error rate reduced from the current baseline to 3.0 percent.
- Rework concentrated in a visible exceptions queue rather than spread through
  the normal flow.
- Errors that do occur are caught at intake, where correction is cheapest.

MEASUREMENT NOTE
Error classification needs an agreed operational definition and appraiser
re-training before the post-implementation numbers can be trusted.
